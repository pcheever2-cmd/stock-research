## FINAL CONSENSUS (23 participants across 9 groups)

### Universally Validated (All Groups)
- 5 questions is the right length (23/23)
- Parenthetical plain-English explanations are the best feature (23/23)
- Company name examples in industry pills are essential (23/23)
- No account/email gate before seeing results (23/23)
- Results must explain WHY each stock matches, not just list them (23/23)
- Position quiz as "starting point" not "prescription" upfront (20/23)
- Show very few stocks (3-6), not a wall of options (19/23)

### Universally Flagged Problems
- "Steady Saver" name is patronizing (flagged by 10+ participants across 7 groups)
- "Moonshot Hunter" is irresponsible/tone-deaf (burned investors, women, older adults)
- Experience question conflates passive 401k with active investing (8+ participants)
- Risk question has implied hierarchy from "scared" to "brave" (Keisha, Rosa, Chris, Tanisha)
- Word "bet" in Q5 is a red flag for 45+ and burned investors (replace with "invest in")
- Quiz doesn't acknowledge that some people are RETURNING, not starting (Chris, Dev, Darnell)
- Quiz doesn't ask about fears/barriers - the actual blocker for many (Nicole, Andre, Dr. Patel)

### The #1 New Insight: The Quiz Solves the Wrong Problem for High Earners
- Dr. Patel ($200k in checking), Nicole ($290k, paralysis), Andre ($185k, opened brokerage 3x)
- Their problem is NOT "what should I invest in" - it's inaction, fear, and overwhelm
- Nicole: "Add one question: 'What's held you back from investing?' That would make me feel more understood than all five current questions combined"
- Andre: "The only way I use this is if quiz results lead to 3 stocks. Not 30. Three."
- Dr. Patel: "I need one button. Not fifteen decisions. One."

### Group-Specific Insights

**Women (Maria, Tanisha, Soo-Jin):**
- Language is neutral but not inclusive - doesn't acknowledge that women's financial lives are often shaped by partners, divorce, caregiving
- "No judgment" on risk question was the best piece of copy (unanimous)
- Shareability: would share profile card (like personality type) but NOT their answers
- Trust: need to know business model transparency, WHO built it, methodology visible

**Burned Investors (Chris, Patricia, Dev):**
- "Moonshot Hunter" is the phrase that cost Chris $12,000 - rename immediately
- "Higher risk, higher reward" is a red flag, not an education point
- "Buying the dip" (Q3 option 4) can be the most dangerous advice for retail investors
- Want the quiz to acknowledge losses are real: "Say the word 'loss'" (Chris)
- Want to see failure cases, not just wins: "Show me what happens when it goes wrong" (Patricia)
- Dev: "If the quiz had one question asking 'Have you lost money investing before?' and changed its tone - that would get my attention"

**Couples (Marcus/Elena, James/Priya):**
- Quiz works for individuals but doesn't address joint decision-making
- Common pattern: one partner invests, the other feels excluded ("just let me handle it")
- Would be useful as a conversation starter between partners with different risk tolerances

**High Earners Who Don't Invest (Dr. Patel, Nicole, Andre):**
- More intimidated than beginners because they're used to being experts
- Problem is inaction, not information - they've over-researched already
- Need absurdly simple next steps: "One stock. One button. Done."
- Andre: "Make my first purchase feel like a celebration, not a risk"
- Nicole: "Stop giving me more information. Start giving me less."
- Dr. Patel: "Respect my time almost aggressively. Show me 3 stocks, not 20."

## Original Detailed Findings (Rounds 1-3)

## Consensus Findings (All Groups Agree)

### 1. Drop the investment amount question
- Feels invasive before delivering value (Priya)
- Conflates wealth with suitability (Marcus)
- "I don't love telling a website how much money I have" (Sarah)

### 2. Add an experience level question
- "The quiz doesn't ask anything about what I already know" (Darnell)
- "Someone with $500 and someone with $50,000 shouldn't get the same results" (Darnell)
- "A beginner who says 'Take a shot' needs very different framing than a veteran" (Marcus)
- "Mike's brand new. We should not get the same experience" (Darnell)

### 3. Industry question needs real company examples
- "If it just said 'Technology sector' I'd be like, what does that mean" (Darnell)
- "The examples are doing the heavy lifting" (Jennifer, Rosa, Darnell all agree)
- "The parenthetical examples are almost defining the category more than the label is" (Jennifer)

### 4. Risk question has aspirational bias
- People pick the "smart" answer, not their real reaction (Raj, Jennifer, Darnell)
- "You think you'd be chill until you're watching it happen on your phone at 2am" (Darnell)
- "I'm answering based on what I think the smart answer is" (Jennifer)

### 5. Answer ordering implies a value judgment
- "They go from scared to brave. The first answer is clearly the one for people who don't know what they're doing" (Keisha)
- "Nobody shares a quiz. They share results" (Mike)

### 6. Profile names have a "coolness hierarchy"
- Moonshot Hunter sounds exciting, Steady Saver feels patronizing
- "Steady Saver sounds like something my grandma would be" (Mike)
- "I'd feel a little insulted" getting Steady Saver (Rosa)
- "Growth Explorer sounds like a kids' show on PBS" (Mike)
- "Moonshot Hunter sounds like a meme stock subreddit username" (Raj)

### 7. Results need to explain WHY
- "If you recommend me a stock, tell me WHY" (Darnell)
- "Show me how the scores are calculated" (Keisha)
- "Let the score be a starting point, not the whole conversation" (Jennifer)
- "Tell me something about myself I didn't realize" (Darnell)

### 8. Position quiz as a starting point, not a prescription
- "Five questions feels too quick to give me a real recommendation" (Rosa)
- "The quiz should be positioned as a starting point, not a destination" (Raj)
- "If a stock site told me 'start with just $200 and build from there' I would trust that infinitely more" (Rosa)

### 9. Trust requires transparency
- "Is this some guy in his basement?" (Mike)
- "Show me how the scoring model would have performed historically" (Raj)
- "Tell me what you CAN'T do. That honesty would go a long way" (Keisha)
- "Don't talk down to me, but also don't assume I know things" (Keisha)

### 10. Shareability requires genuine insight, not labels
- "Nobody shares a quiz. They share results" (Mike)
- "People share things that feel personal" (Darnell)
- "If it felt like someone actually built this for someone like me, I'd tell every teacher in my department" (Keisha)
- Shareable result card that looks good on Instagram/iMessage (Sarah)

## Specific Question-Level Feedback

### Q1 (Main Goal)
- Options aren't mutually exclusive - real investors have blended goals (Raj, Jennifer)
- "Build long-term wealth" and "keep savings safe" sound almost the same to beginners (Sarah)
- Missing: "I've been investing poorly and want to improve" (Darnell)
- Parenthetical explainers are valued by everyone

### Q2 (Risk / $1,000 Drop)
- "Stressed and want to pull out" conflates feeling stress with actually selling (Rosa)
- Need option for "stressed but would hold" (Rosa, multiple agree)
- $1,000 example is good but how you react depends on total portfolio size (Jennifer, Raj)
- 4 options instead of 3 helps differentiate profiles (Marcus)

### Q3 (Time Horizon)
- "5+ years" is too broad - collapses 5 years and 25 years (Jennifer, Raj)
- "I just want to get started" feels different from "no timeline" (Darnell)
- Missing: distinction between "extra money" and "money I might need" (Mike)

### Q4 (Industries)
- Categories too broad - "Tech" covers Apple and some random AI startup (Darnell, Mike)
- Company examples are essential - keep them (universal agreement)
- "Open to anything" feels like a wasted answer (Rosa)
- Asking what you're interested in != what you need (Raj)
- Pills with icons + examples is the right format (Priya)

### Q5 (Investment Style)
- "On sale" is jargon that means different things to different people (Rosa, Mike, Raj)
- "You're making value investing sound like shopping at TJ Maxx" (Raj)
- "A little bit of everything" is the wishy-washy escape hatch (Darnell)

## Design/UX Consensus

- **5 questions max** for completion rate (Priya)
- **Auto-advance** on single-select, explicit Continue on multi-select (Priya)
- **One question per screen** with segmented progress bar (all)
- **Mobile full-screen takeover** (Priya)
- **Results page is the star** - needs to be screenshot-worthy (all)
- **Horizontal slide transitions**, 300ms (Priya)
- **"Smart friend" tone** - not BuzzFeed, not Bloomberg (Priya)
- **Compliance disclaimers** - "Explore" not "Recommended" (Marcus)
